\problem You are trying to model daily average calorie proportion in people's diet from ultra high processed foods (UHPFs). Since it's a proportion, the response is $\mathcal{Y} = [0,1]$. Typically, such responses are modeled with the beta rv. We will reparameterize it below from what you saw in MATH 340:

\beqn
Y \sim \betanot{\mu}{\phi} := \frac{1}{B(\mu\phi, (1 - \mu)\phi)} \, y^{\mu\phi - 1} (1 - y)^{(1 - \mu)\phi - 1}\indic{y > 0},\quad \expe{Y} = \mu
\eeqn

\noindent This way we can model its expectation directly. The $\phi$ parameter is then a nuisance parameter controlling the variance (the thinness of the PDF's bell shape).

There is another wrinkle: some people are very healthy and eat no UHPFs; some people are extremely unhealthy and eat all UHPFs and everything in between. Thus the realized values of the $y_i$'s in the survey will have 0, 1's and any fractions between 0 and 1. Thus we need to model exact 0's and exact 1's. We can do that with two more parameters:

\beqn
Y_i \inddist \begin{cases}
0  & w.p.~~~ \theta_0 \\
\betanot{\mu}{\phi} & w.p.~~~ 1 - \theta_0 - \theta_1 \\
1  & w.p.~~~ \theta_1
\end{cases}
\eeqn

\noindent This model is known as the \textit{zero-one inflated beta model}. Also, we have covariates that we care about modeling the effect for each individual $i$:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$x_{i,1}$}: A continuous metric called the Household Income-to-Poverty Ratio
    \item \textbf{$x_{i,2}$}: A continuous metric called the Food Desert Index / Neighborhood Food Environment which is geographic proximity to supermarkets selling healthy food
    \item \textbf{$x_{i,3}$}: A binary variable assessing whether or not WIC/SNAP enrolled
    \item \textbf{$x_{i,4}$}: Average weekly working hours scaled over 40
    \item \textbf{$x_{i,5}$}: Continuous metric of age in years scaled over 120
    \item \textbf{$x_{i,6}$}: Continuous metric of body-to-mass-index scaled over 30
    \item \textbf{$x_{i,7}$}: Binary variable assessing the presence of a comorbidity such as diabetes, high blood pressure, etc.
\end{itemize}


\noindent We would like to model the mean of the non-zero, non-one proportion with these features. But we do not model the proportion of zeroes and ones with these features. So we choose a linear model and we use a logit link function just like in logistic regression:

\beqn
\mu_i = \phi(\x_i\bbeta) = \frac{e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 x_{i,1} + \ldots + \beta_7 x_{i,7}}}{1 + e^{\beta_0 + \beta_1 x_{i,1} + \ldots + \beta_7 x_{i,7}}} = \oneover{1 + e^{-\x_i\bbeta}}, \quad 1 - \mu_i = \oneover{1 + e^{\x_i\bbeta}}
\eeqn
